Lantern Light Across the Stormy Lake

30 October 2026 · mysterious · fictional sample

You were sent to Camp Wahkeena on Pine Lake for the summer when you turned twelve. On the last night, you and your friend snuck down to the dock and saw a small yellow light drifting across the water.

People
Danny (counselor), Mr. Abbott (caretaker), Biscuit (dog), Maya (friend)
Places
Camp Wahkeena, Pine Lake
Keepsakes
old leather dog collar

The words as they were spoken

The summer I turned twelve, my parents sent me to Camp Wahkeena, up on Pine Lake. On the last night, our counselor Danny built the campfire extra high and told us about the lantern. He said an old caretaker named Mr. Abbott used to row across the lake every night with a lantern, calling for his dog, Biscuit. One stormy night the boat came back empty, and ever since, if you sat by the water after midnight, you could see his light moving across the lake. Everybody laughed. But later, my friend Maya and I snuck down to the dock, and there it was. A small yellow light, low on the water, drifting toward the far shore. We didn't say a word. In the morning there was an old leather dog collar lying on the dock, still wet. I kept it. Danny swore he had nothing to do with it, and I never found out who did.

A haunted retelling

A retelling by the Archivist, not the memory itself.

The wind whips cold tonight. 🔥 Listen close. 🌲 It was a summer many years past. My dear, twelve years old. Pine Lake. Camp Wahkeena. 🌙 The last night felt heavy. The counselors spoke of a lantern. Mr. Abbott. He rowed across the black water, calling for his dog, Biscuit. 👻 They said the boat returned empty once. And that light, it never truly left. So, Maya and I crept down to the dock. Under the shadow of the pines. There it was. A small, lonely yellow spark. Drifting across the vast, dark water. We stayed silent. A breath held tight in the cold air. When morning broke, a soaked leather collar lay there. On the wood. I kept it. No one ever knew who left it. But the light still moves sometimes. 🕯️ It waits for someone watching the dark.

The Scarecrow's Visit on Halloween Night

30 October 2026 · nostalgic · fictional sample

You and your little brother made a plan to stay up and watch the scarecrow, Old Hollis, on Halloween night. In the morning, you found Old Hollis sitting on the porch swing, wearing a red wool scarf and with a mug of cocoa beside him.

People
Grandpa Joe, Sam, Grandma
Places
Iowa
When
1996
Keepsakes
red wool scarf, mug of cocoa

The words as they were spoken

Every Halloween we drove out to Grandpa Joe's farm in Iowa, and every Halloween he told us the same story. He said the scarecrow in the back field, the one he called Old Hollis, got cold on October nights and walked up to the porch to sit by the fire. My little brother Sam believed every word. So one year, I think it was 1996, Sam and I made a plan to stay up and catch him. We sat by the window with a flashlight and a bag of candy corn, and of course we fell asleep. In the morning, Old Hollis was sitting on the porch swing, wearing Grandpa's red wool scarf, with a mug of cocoa next to him. Grandpa swore up and down he never touched him. Grandma just smiled and refilled the mug. I still have that scarf. It still smells a little like wood smoke.

A retelling by the Archivist, not the memory itself.

The wind whistles low tonight. 🌬️ Hear this memory. It came to me from the quiet spaces between heartbeats. It was Halloween night. 🎃 The shadows danced long across the Iowa fields. A flashlight beam cut the blackness. Candy corn sat beside us, untouched. 🍬 Sleep found us soon. When dawn crept in, the porch held a strange stillness. There he was. Old Hollis. Upon the swing. He wore Grandpa's red wool scarf. 🔥 And by his worn boots, a mug of cocoa steamed faintly. Grandma smiled. She just smiled. And refilled the mug. 🌙 That scarf remains. Halloween Power Outage in Duluth

30 October 2026 · joyful · fictional sample

You remember the power going out on your whole street in Duluth during trick-or-treating on Halloween in 2003. Your dad lined the porch steps with jack-o'-lanterns and told ghost stories to the kids who came by while your mom passed around cocoa.

People
dad, mom
Places
Duluth
When
2003
Keepsakes
jack-o'-lanterns, cocoa

The words as they were written

On Halloween in 2003 the power went out on our whole street in Duluth, right when the trick-or-treaters came out. My dad lined the porch steps with jack-o'-lanterns and told a ghost story to every kid who came by, and by nine o'clock there were twenty kids sitting on our lawn in the dark. My mom passed around cocoa in paper cups. When the lights came back on, everybody groaned. Dad still says it was the best Halloween our street ever had.

Scarf with a Note from Grandma Ruth

30 October 2026 · nostalgic · fictional sample

You recall that your grandma Ruth knitted each of you a scarf every winter, and she would hide a folded note inside each one. In the year she died, you found one last scarf in her cedar chest with your name pinned to it, and the note inside simply said, "still here."

People
Grandma Ruth
Keepsakes
scarf, folded note

The words as they were written

Every winter my grandma Ruth knitted each of us a scarf, and she always hid a tiny pocket inside with a folded note. Mine usually said, be brave, little owl. The year she died, I found one last scarf in her cedar chest with my name pinned to it. The note in the pocket just said, still here. I wear it every Halloween, because she loved the cold.

Rainy Night Encounter on Route 9

30 October 2026 · eerie · fictional sample

You recall an instance where a girl in a white raincoat was seen by a mile marker on Route 9 during heavy rain. After picking her up, you noticed the seat was wet when you arrived at her street, and the seat was empty.

People
Uncle Ray
Places
Route 9, Millbrook, the Hollow
When
childhood
Keepsakes
photograph

The words as they were spoken

My Uncle Ray swears, this happened on Route 9, just outside of Millbrook, back when he drove a rusty blue pickup. It was raining hard and around two in the morning he saw a girl in a white raincoat standing by the mile marker. He pulled over and asked if she needed a ride. She said she was going home to see the Hollow and didn't say another word the whole way. When he turned onto her street and looked over the seat was empty but the window was rolled down and the seat was soaking wet. The next day he went back to the house and an old woman answered the door. She showed him a photograph of her daughter in that same white raincoat. Uncle Ray still won't drive Route 9 after dark.
